Monday MovesMonday Moves

One move, every room, five minutes. Thirty-six small moves, one for each week of the school year, that give English learners access to grade-level work in any subject. Each one has three steps, examples by department, and a version for each of the four lanes.

Una estrategia, cada salón, cinco minutos. Treinta y seis estrategias breves, una para cada semana del año escolar, que dan a los estudiantes de inglés acceso al trabajo del grado en cualquier materia. Cada una tiene tres pasos, ejemplos por departamento y una versión para cada uno de los cuatro carriles.

How to use Monday MovesCómo usar Monday Moves

These cards are written for classroom teachers of every subject, not only for English teachers. An EL specialist or instructional coach can send one each Monday, post it in the workroom, or open a team meeting with it. Each quarter has a printable nine-page packet. Select any move below to open it.

Estas tarjetas están escritas para docentes de todas las materias, no solo de inglés. Un especialista en estudiantes de inglés o un asesor pedagógico puede enviar una cada lunes, publicarla en la sala de maestros o abrir con ella una reunión de equipo. Cada trimestre tiene un paquete imprimible de nueve páginas. Selecciona cualquier estrategia para abrirla.

1

Post and Say the Language GoalPublica y di el objetivo de lenguaje

Students hit targets they can see.Los estudiantes alcanzan las metas que pueden ver.

Why it works

A content objective tells students what they will learn. A language goal tells them what they will have to say or write to prove it. English learners are graded on both every day, but usually only the first one is posted. Naming the second takes one line on the board.

How to do it

  1. Start with your content objective.“Students will identify the causes of the Dust Bowl.”
  2. Ask what students must say or write to show it.They will have to explain causes, so they need because and as a result.
  3. Write it with a function verb and the language.“Explain two causes using because and as a result.” Then read it aloud.

In your subject

ELAExplain the theme using “The theme is that …”
MathJustify your answer using because and therefore.
ScienceDescribe the results using increased, decreased, and stayed the same.
Social studiesCompare two causes using both and whereas.
CTE / electivesSequence the procedure using first, next, and finally.

Same move, four lanes

NewcomerRecién llegado

Points to the posted goal and repeats the key word.

EmergingEmergente

Uses the target word in a short frame.

ProgressingEn progreso

Uses the target structure in a full sentence.

ChallengeDesafío

Uses it twice, in two different sentence patterns.

Try it this weekAdd a language goal to one lesson a day this week. Function verbs to choose from: describe, explain, compare, sequence, justify.
What a visitor would seeA second line under the objective that names a word or sentence pattern, and a teacher who says it out loud.
2

Preview Three WordsAnticipa tres palabras

Thirty seconds that save ten minutes.Treinta segundos que ahorran diez minutos.

Why it works

Students cannot use a word they have never said aloud. Choosing three words and having everyone say them before the lesson removes the biggest barrier to participation. For Spanish speakers, many academic words are already half-known: they are cognates.

How to do it

  1. Choose three words students must SAY today.Not the hardest words in the text. The ones they need for their answers.
  2. Say it, show it, connect it.Say the word, show a picture or gesture, and give the Spanish cognate if there is one.
  3. Everyone says it twice.Once to the room, once in a sentence to a partner.

In your subject

ELAevidence / evidencia · narrator / narrador · theme / tema
Mathequation / ecuación · variable / variable · solution / solución
Sciencecell / célula · energy / energía · hypothesis / hipótesis
Social studiesconstitution / constitución · migration / migración · democracy / democracia
Watch for false friendsexit is not éxito (success) · library is not librería (bookstore) · carpet is not carpeta (folder)

3

Hand Over a Sentence FrameEntrega un marco de oración

A frame is a ramp, not a crutch.Un marco es una rampa, no una muleta.

Why it works

Most students, not only English learners, know more than they can put into an academic sentence. A frame supplies the structure so their thinking can show. Post one frame for the whole class; it raises the quality of every answer and singles no one out.

How to do it

  1. Decide what kind of thinking you are asking for.Describing, explaining a cause, comparing, justifying, or disagreeing.
  2. Post the frame that matches.One frame on the board before the question is asked.
  3. Fade it.Offer it all week, then cover it and see who still uses the pattern.

What it looks like

DescribeThe ___ has ___ and ___. · I notice that ___.
Explain a cause___ happened because ___. · As a result, ___.
CompareBoth ___ and ___ are ___, but ___ is ___.
JustifyI know this because the text / data / diagram shows ___.
Disagree politelyI see it differently because ___. · Another way to look at it is ___.

Same move, four lanes

NewcomerRecién llegado

Completes the frame with one word or a label.

EmergingEmergente

Completes the frame with a phrase from a word bank.

ProgressingEn progreso

Uses the frame and adds a second sentence.

ChallengeDesafío

Drops the frame and keeps the structure.

Try it this weekChoose one frame and use it in every class for three days. On the fourth day, take it down and listen for it.
What a visitor would seeA sentence frame on the board and students starting their answers with it.
4

Talk Before WritingHablar antes de escribir

If they can say it, they can write it.Si pueden decirlo, pueden escribirlo.

Why it works

Writing is the hardest language skill, and we often ask for it first. Sixty seconds of talk lets students find the words, hear a model from a partner, and fix mistakes while the stakes are low. The writing that follows is longer and more accurate.

How to do it

  1. Ask the question, then give silent think time.Ten seconds. Nobody talks, nobody writes.
  2. Partners talk for sixty seconds.Home language is welcome here. The goal is the idea.
  3. Then write, using the frame.Partners can repeat each other’s best sentence. That is learning, not copying.

In your subject

ELA“Tell your partner the theme in one sentence before you write your paragraph.”
Math“Explain to your partner how you got your answer before you write the steps.”
Science“Predict what will happen and tell your partner why, then write your hypothesis.”
Social studies“Tell your partner which cause mattered most. Then write your claim.”
Any class“Say your first sentence out loud. Now write exactly what you said.”

EmergingEmergente

Check Without Asking “Do You Understand?”Comprueba sin preguntar «¿Entienden?»

Everyone says yes. Ask them to show you.Todos dicen que sí. Pídeles que te lo demuestren.

Why it works

In many cultures, telling a teacher “I don’t understand” is disrespectful, and nodding is polite. “Any questions?” tells you almost nothing. A check that asks every student to do something visible takes the same ten seconds and tells you the truth.

How to do it

  1. Replace the question with a task.“Show me,” “point to,” “hold up,” “tell your partner.”
  2. Make every student respond at once.Whiteboards, fingers, cards, or a partner turn.
  3. Act on what you see.Reteach to the five who missed it while the rest begin.

What it looks like

Show meWhiteboards up: write the next step, the answer, or one word.
Point to it“Put your finger on the sentence that proves it.”
Sort itTwo cards, two piles: example or non-example.
Fingers“One finger for A, two for B.” Then: “Tell your partner why.”
Say it back“Tell your partner the directions in your own words.”

Make the Text ReachableHaz alcanzable el texto

Keep the text. Change the path to it.Conserva el texto. Cambia el camino para llegar a él.

Why it works

Giving English learners an easier text feels kind, but it means they never practice the reading the test and the next grade require. The better move is to keep the grade-level text and lower the barriers around it: smaller pieces, clearer signposts, and a few words explained.

How to do it

  1. Chunk it and number it.Number the paragraphs. Assign one chunk at a time with one question each.
  2. Front-load five words and one picture.A short glossary in the margin, and a photo, map, or diagram for background.
  3. Read the question first, aloud.Students read with a purpose when they know what they are looking for.

In your subject

ELANumber the paragraphs. Ask one question per section instead of ten at the end.
MathRead the word problem aloud. Students underline the question and circle the numbers.
SciencePair the lab procedure with a labeled diagram of each step.
Social studiesGive a timeline or map before the primary source, and gloss five archaic words.
Any classLet students annotate in either language. Notes are thinking, not the product.

NewcomerRecién llegado

7

Grade the Content, Grow the EnglishCalifica el contenido, haz crecer el inglés

Two different questions. Two different marks.Dos preguntas distintas. Dos notas distintas.

Why it works

When a newcomer’s correct science answer loses points for grammar, the grade stops telling the truth about science. Separating content from language keeps grades honest and still holds students accountable for growing their English, one target at a time.

How to do it

  1. Score the content first.Did the student show the concept, by any means: words, labels, a diagram, or talk?
  2. Give language feedback on one target.Choose one pattern, such as past-tense -ed, and mark only that.
  3. Let the lane decide the format, not the standard.Same concept, different ways of showing it.

What it looks like

RubricAdd two lines: “Content understanding” and “Academic language.” Weight content more.
Feedback“Your claim is correct. This week, check every verb for -ed.”
AlternativesLabeled diagram, sorted cards, recorded explanation, or a conference at your desk.
AvoidZeros for blank written answers from newcomers. Ask them to show it another way first.
RememberA student’s plan may list grading accommodations. If you are unsure, ask your EL specialist.

Test-Ready All YearListos para el examen todo el año

A support met on test day is not a support.Un apoyo que se conoce el día del examen no es un apoyo.

Why it works

English learners are entitled to the testing supports written in their language service plans, but an unfamiliar tool costs time instead of saving it. Students do best when the supports, the question language, and the screen are already routine.

How to do it

  1. Know each student’s listed supports.They are in the language service plan. Ask your EL specialist for any you do not have.
  2. Use them in ordinary classwork.Same tools, same way, every unit, so nothing is new in the spring.
  3. Teach the language of the questions.Words like best, most likely, primarily, and except decide the answer.

What it looks like

Question wordsbest · most likely · primarily · according to · except · which statement supports
Two-part itemsPart A asks for an answer. Part B asks for the proof. Teach students to check that they match.
Select two“Select two” means exactly two. Practice it in class quizzes.
StaminaBuild up to long sittings gradually. Teach how to take a short break and return.
Online toolsUse the state’s practice tests so the highlighter, notepad, and navigation are familiar.

Families and WinsFamilias y logros

The first call home should be good news.La primera llamada a casa debe ser una buena noticia.

Why it works

Many of our families want to be involved and are unsure they are welcome, especially when no one at school speaks their language. One short positive contact changes that, and it changes how a student walks into your room the next day.

How to do it

  1. Make one positive contact.A two-sentence note or call about something the student did well.
  2. Use an adult interpreter or a translated note.Never the student, and never a sibling, for anything about grades or behavior.
  3. Tell families one thing to do.“Ask your child to explain today’s lesson in Spanish.” That is real homework help.

What it looks like

Say it“Buenas tardes. Soy el maestro / la maestra de ___. Llamo con buenas noticias.”
The news“___ trabajó muy bien esta semana. Estoy orgulloso / orgullosa de su esfuerzo.”
The ask“Pídale que le explique en español lo que aprendió hoy.”
Close“Gracias por su apoyo. Estoy a sus órdenes.”
Need help?Ask your EL specialist or a district interpreter to translate the message or make the call with you.

ProgressingEn progreso

Celebrate: first answer given without the frame.

ChallengeDesafío

Go on a Cognate HuntSal a cazar cognados

Thousands of words your students already know.Miles de palabras que tus estudiantes ya conocen.

Why it works

About a third of academic English has a close relative in Spanish. Students do not notice cognates automatically; they have to be taught to look. Once they do, a page of science or history text stops looking foreign.

How to do it

  1. Teach the three big endings.-tion is -ción, -ity is -idad, -ly is -mente: nation, nación; activity, actividad; rapidly, rápidamente.
  2. Hunt before reading.Give students two minutes to circle every word that looks like Spanish.
  3. Check the trap.Ask, “Does it mean the same thing?” Most do. A few do not.

In your subject

ELAnarrator / narrador · conflict / conflicto · metaphor / metáfora
Mathtriangle / triángulo · fraction / fracción · multiply / multiplicar
Scienceorganism / organismo · gravity / gravedad · reaction / reacción
Social studiescolony / colonia · revolution / revolución · economy / economía
False friendsactual means real, not current · sensible means reasonable, not sensitive · embarrassed is not embarazada

Word Walls That WorkMuros de palabras que funcionan

If nobody points at it, it is wallpaper.Si nadie lo señala, es papel tapiz.

Why it works

A word wall helps only when students use it while they talk and write. The walls that work are small, current, visual, and touched every day. Twelve words students actually use beat sixty they never look at.

How to do it

  1. Keep it to this unit.Ten to fifteen words. Take the old ones down.
  2. Add a picture and a cognate to each.A quick sketch counts. Let students make the cards.
  3. Make students use it.“Use one wall word in your answer.” Point to it when you say it.

What it looks like

Organize by meaningGroup words that go together: processes, tools, people, results.
Add the word’s partnersevidence: strong evidence, provide evidence, evidence shows
Make it reachableLow enough to read from every seat, in print large enough to copy.
Give it a jobExit tickets must include two wall words, underlined.
Retire wordsMove mastered words to a “We own these” list. Students love watching it grow.

Teach the Word PartsEnseña las partes de las palabras

One root unlocks twenty words.Una raíz abre veinte palabras.

Why it works

Students meet thousands of new academic words a year, far more than anyone can teach one at a time. Word parts are the shortcut, and most Greek and Latin roots are shared with Spanish, so English learners often have a head start.

How to do it

  1. Pick the part that repeats in your subject.One prefix, root, or suffix per week is plenty.
  2. Build a family.Write the part in the middle and let students add every word they know.
  3. Predict, then check.“If geo means earth, what might geothermal mean?”

In your subject

ELAauto (self), bio (life), graph (write): autobiography
Mathpoly (many), equi (equal), meter (measure): equilateral, perimeter
Sciencephoto (light), hydro (water), therm (heat): photosynthesis
Social studiesdemo (people), cracy (rule), migr (move): democracy, migrate
Any classun-, re-, pre-, dis-, -tion, -able cover a large share of all prefixed and suffixed words.

Read the Question FirstLee primero la pregunta

Reading with a purpose is a different activity.Leer con un propósito es otra actividad.

Why it works

Strong readers know what they are looking for before they begin. Many English learners read every word at the same slow speed because nobody told them what mattered. Reading the question first tells them where to spend their effort.

How to do it

  1. Read the question aloud before the text.Students underline what it asks them to find.
  2. Have them restate it.“Tell your partner what you are looking for.”
  3. Read, then return to the question.Students mark the place in the text where they found it.

In your subject

ELA“You are reading to find out how Tomás changes. Mark where he changes.”
Math“Underline the question in the word problem. Circle what you are solving for.”
Science“Read to find two causes of erosion. Number them in the text.”
Social studies“Read to find out why the author opposed the law. Mark his reasons.”
Any classPut the question at the top of the page, not the bottom.

Annotate with a PurposeAnota con un propósito

Three marks, not a rainbow.Tres marcas, no un arcoíris.

Why it works

Highlighting everything is the same as highlighting nothing. A short, fixed set of marks gives students something to do while they read and gives you a window into their thinking. Notes in the home language are welcome; they are notes, not the final product.

How to do it

  1. Teach three marks.A question mark for confusion, a star for important, an arrow for connections.
  2. Model on the first paragraph.Think aloud for one minute while you mark.
  3. Talk from the marks.“Find one question mark and ask your partner about it.”

What it looks like

? confusionA word or sentence I do not understand yet.
★ importantSomething that answers the question I was given.
→ connectionThis connects to another part, another class, or my own life.
Margin notesTwo or three words, in any language. Translations of key words are fine.
After readingQuestion marks become the class discussion. Nothing is more useful to you.

Match the Organizer to the ThinkingAjusta el organizador al pensamiento

The shape of the chart is the shape of the idea.La forma del esquema es la forma de la idea.

Why it works

A graphic organizer lets students show complex thinking with very little language. It only helps when its shape matches the thinking the task requires. The wrong organizer adds confusion instead of removing it.

How to do it

  1. Name the thinking.Comparing? Sequencing? Cause and effect? Claim and evidence?
  2. Choose the matching shape.Use the same shape every time for that kind of thinking.
  3. Turn the chart into sentences.Each box becomes a sentence with the week’s frame.

What it looks like

CompareTwo columns or overlapping circles: both … , but …
SequenceA chain of boxes with arrows: first, next, finally
Cause and effectArrow from one box to another: because … , as a result …
Claim and evidenceOne box on top, three underneath: I claim … , the evidence is …
Main ideaA table top with legs: the main idea is … , one detail is …

Read Aloud, Think AloudLee en voz alta, piensa en voz alta

Let them hear what a reader’s mind does.Que oigan lo que hace la mente de un lector.

Why it works

Students see the result of good reading but never the process. Two minutes of reading aloud while saying your thoughts shows them what to do when a sentence is hard. It also lets English learners hear how academic English sounds.

How to do it

  1. Choose the hardest paragraph.Not the whole text. Two minutes.
  2. Read it aloud and stop three times.“I’m not sure what that word means, so I’ll look at the next sentence.”
  3. Hand it over.Partners do the same with the next paragraph.

In your subject

ELA“The author says ‘noble.’ That seems too big. I think this is sarcasm.”
Math“It says ‘at least.’ That means this number or more, so I need the ≥ sign.”
Science“The graph goes up and then flat. I wonder what stopped the growth.”
Social studies“This was written in 1863, so ‘the war’ must mean the Civil War.”
Any className the strategy: reread, look around the word, check the picture, break it into parts.

Build Background in Two MinutesConstruye contexto en dos minutos

You cannot comprehend what you cannot picture.No se comprende lo que no se puede imaginar.

Why it works

A reading about the Dust Bowl is hard if you have never seen a prairie, however strong your English is. Much of what looks like a language problem is a background problem. Two minutes with an image fixes more than a vocabulary list does.

How to do it

  1. Find one image, map, or thirty-second clip.Show it before the reading.
  2. Ask what students notice and what it reminds them of.Answers in any language count.
  3. Connect it to what they already know.Their home countries have droughts, migrations, elections, and rivers too.

In your subject

ELAA photograph of the setting before a story set somewhere unfamiliar.
MathA real receipt, pay stub, or floor plan before a word problem about one.
ScienceA short clip of the phenomenon before the explanation of it.
Social studiesA map and a timeline before any primary source.
Any class“Has anyone seen something like this? Where?” Then listen.

Look at the Work TogetherMiren juntos el trabajo

Why it works

English learners often settle into short, safe sentences because those do not get marked wrong. Growth comes from joining and stretching them. Sentence combining is one of the few grammar practices with strong evidence that it improves writing.

How to do it

  1. Start with two short sentences from your content.“The river rose. The levee held.”
  2. Join them three ways.and / but / so · because / although / when · which / who
  3. Choose the best one for the meaning.Discuss why. That conversation is the grammar lesson.

In your subject

Science“The ice melted. The water level stayed the same.” → “When the ice melted, …”
Social studies“The law passed. Many people opposed it.” → “The law passed, although …”
